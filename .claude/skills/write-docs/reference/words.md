# Words and patterns to remove

Strike these in the last pass of a draft. Each entry says what to do in its
place.

## Words

- Inflated words: crucial, pivotal, robust, seamless, powerful,
  comprehensive, landscape, leverage, delve, showcase, underscore,
  testament, cutting-edge, game-changer, unlock, empower, elevate, harness.
  Say what the thing does, with its number where there is one.
- Softeners and intensifiers: simply, just, easily, really, very, quite,
  actually, basically, of course, obviously.
- Puffed verbs: "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features", "offers".
  Use "is", "are" or "has".
- Spare synonyms: a second word for a concept that `docs/core-semantics.md`'s
  Terminology already names. Use that section's word.

## Patterns

- Staging phrases that announce the content: "It's worth noting that",
  "Let's take a look at", "In this section we will", "Here's the thing".
  Start with the content.
- Closers that repeat the paragraph: "In short, …", "In summary, …", "This
  means that…" followed by what the paragraph said. Cut them.
- Sayings that sound deep and state nothing: "Correctness is a journey",
  "Data you can trust". Replace them with the fact they gesture at, or cut.
- Riders on a fact: "…, which makes it ideal for production", "…,
  ensuring peace of mind". Keep the fact and cut the rider, or state the
  consequence as its own claim with its support.
- Triads for rhythm: three adjectives or three clauses where one carries the
  meaning ("fast, safe and reliable"). Keep the one that is true and linked.
- Stacked hedges: "may potentially", "could possibly", "in some cases it
  might". State the condition under which it holds.
- Opposing pairs: "X, not Y", "proved, not tested", "instead of". State X.
- Vague links: "click here", "this page", "see more", a bare URL in prose.
  Name the target in the link text.
- Chat residue: "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Certainly",
  "Note:" as a reflex, first-person "I" in user docs.
- Text about the page: "This page explains…", "As mentioned above",
  "Below you will find". The headings and the content say it.
- Dashes in prose for asides or emphasis. Use a comma, colon or
  parentheses.
- A colon list or bold label for every short paragraph. Write the paragraph.
